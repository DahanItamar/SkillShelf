# AI Interaction and Content Disclosure

EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations **became applicable on 2 August 2026**. Anything
already shipped is in scope now, not later.

## 1. Interaction disclosure — before the first exchange

Shown at the interface, not in terms. Visible without expanding anything.

**Chat or assistant entry point**

> You are talking to an AI assistant, not a person. It can make mistakes — check anything
> important. To reach a human, use [INSERT_CONTACT_EMAIL].

**Compact form, where space is tight**

> AI assistant · can make mistakes

**Voice interface — spoken before the conversation starts**

> Hello. You are speaking with an automated assistant, not a person.

The exemption for cases obvious to a reasonably informed person is narrow. It is not a
defence you want to argue, and it costs one line to avoid needing it.

## 2. Generated content disclosure

**Beside generated media**

> Generated by AI

**Beside AI-assisted text published to inform the public**

> This text was produced with AI assistance and reviewed by a person before publishing.

Article 50 paragraph 4 requires a disclosure on deep fakes and on AI-generated text published
to inform the public on matters of public interest. That one has to be visible, not only in
metadata.

## 3. Machine-readable marking

The visible label is not enough on its own. Synthetic audio, image, video and text must carry a
machine-readable mark that it was artificially generated or manipulated.

- Attach provenance **at the point of generation**, not at upload or export.
- Use an interoperable format. Content Credentials, following the C2PA specification, is the
  one with cross-platform support.
- Assume stripping. Most social platforms remove embedded metadata on upload, so the mark has
  to be the file's own rather than a wrapper around it.
- Keep the mark through your own pipeline. Resizing, transcoding and thumbnailing all drop
  metadata unless the tooling is configured to preserve it.

## 4. Longer notice for a privacy policy or a help page

> **How we use AI in [INSERT_SERVICE_NAME]**
>
> Some features of this service use artificial intelligence. We tell you when you are
> interacting with an AI system rather than a person, and we mark content our systems generate.
>
> **What the AI does here.** It answers questions, drafts text, and summarises information you
> give it.
>
> **What it does not do.** It does not make decisions about you that produce legal effects or
> similarly significant effects without a person involved.
>
> **Where it runs.** We use a model provided by [INSERT_MODEL_PROVIDER]. Content you send to
> an AI feature is transmitted to that provider to produce a response.
>
> **What we do with your input.** We use it to produce your response and to operate the
> service. We do not use it to train models unless you have told us we may.
>
> **It can be wrong.** AI systems produce plausible text that is sometimes inaccurate. Check
> anything you rely on.
>
> **Reaching a person.** Contact [INSERT_CONTACT_EMAIL] at any time.
>
> [INSERT_COMPANY_NAME]

## Notes for the drafter — delete before publishing

- **Hebrew is required** if you serve Israeli consumers, even though this obligation is an EU
  one — the disclosure has to be understood by the person reading it.
- The paragraph saying the AI makes no decisions with legal or similarly significant effects
  must be true. If it screens applications, prices individually, or moderates accounts, that
  is a different and much heavier part of the AI Act, and this template does not cover it.
- The statement about training on user input must match your provider contract. Check the
  actual terms rather than the marketing page.
- Article 50 is the transparency layer only. Systems in the high-risk categories carry
  obligations this template does not touch.
